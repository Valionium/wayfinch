# Rule register

One line per rule, in short words, ending in its ID. `npm run rules` reads this file; a test whose title starts with `[ID]` proves the rule. Section numbers are those of the design notes. IDs listed as `later:` in `tests/rules-in-force.txt` are planned and not yet proved by a test.

## Place pack format

- A tile written and read back gives the same places (place packs) [PIPE-FMT-01]
- Writing the same data twice gives the same bytes (place packs) [PIPE-FMT-02]
- Every place lies in exactly one tile (place packs) [PIPE-TILE-01]
- A tile never holds more than 5,000 places (place packs) [PIPE-TILE-02]

- A position projects onto the route with distance along it taken from the haversine totals (§6.1) [LOC-PROJECT]

- More than 5 km from the route sets the far-away flag (§6.1) [LOC-FAR]

## Climbs and climbing card

- A climb needs 100 m at 3 percent; close rises merge across a dip under 20 m; it starts at its foot and ends at its top, so a flat or a valley road rising under 0.5 percent is no part of it, 6 km of flat ends it as a dip does, and a rise averaging under 3 percent keeps its steeper part as the climb, on files with a point every few metres as on sparse ones (§6.7; review of PR 150, C1) [CL-CLIMB]
- Climbing left is told to the stop and the end with the next climb (§6.7) [CL-LEFT]
- Missing heights read unknown, never zero metres (§6.7) [CL-UNKNOWN]

## Climb check (the owner's idea of 14 Nov 2026, design 2026-11-14-climb-check-design.md)

- a route with a missing height gets no climbs and no guess; Climb check says it needs the heights and that Prepare this route looks them up [CC-NOHEIGHT]
- a route with heights and no climb (CL-CLIMB: 100 m at 3%) says so plainly; a stored climb with a broken figure is left out [CC-NONE]
- the climbs are listed hardest first (gain times average grade), at most 10, the rest counted; each with its day, km from and to, length, metres up, average grade and steepest 500 m [CC-LIST]
- each climb's time is the pace engine's riding time at the trip's flat speed with a loaded bike, without breaks, shown to 5 min as an estimate; a speed that gives no time gives none [CC-TIME]
- by the steepest 500 m: under 8% steady, from 8% steep, from 12% likely a walk in places with a loaded bike [CC-WALK]
- a bike with a motor (Bike details' e-bike) gets no walk judgement, and the list says why [CC-EBIKE]
- a climb over a night names its days ("Days 1 to 2") and says where each day ends on it, as Trip's days do (review of PR 150, I1) [CC-DAYS]
- the steepest 500 m (here and on En route's Climbing card) is read on the heights averaged over 500 m, never under the climb's average, so recording noise or a bridge in the heights barely moves it; the lead says such heights can make a stretch look steeper (review of PR 150, I2) [CC-STEEP]
- every figure says it is an estimate and that short ramps can be steeper; no gear and no health advice [CC-HONEST]
- on Trip, Climb check is one button under the days in the route summary, folded until opened; its list is its own chunk, opens offline, and works by keyboard at 320 px in both themes [CC-TRIP]

## Pace

- A detour adds its one-way time at the place and its round trip to everything later (§6.2, §6.12) [PACE-DETOUR]

- Today's stop is the end of the stage the day start lies in, never beyond the route end (§6.2, §6.2a) [PACE-STOPRULE]
- A later day starts at the previous stop at the daily start time, or at a late arrival (§6.2, §6.2a) [PACE-LATER]

- The pace correction scales the rest of today but not tomorrow's ride from its departure (§6.2, §6.2a) [PACE-R-LATER]
- Every caller gets the same unrounded arrival for the same place and departure (§6.2a) [PACE-ONE-TIME]
